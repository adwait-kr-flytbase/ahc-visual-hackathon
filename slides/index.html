What we built

A 4B model watches the video
and raises the alert itself.

Real-time video anomaly detection · 11 classes
Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct · one A100-40GB
34 public test videos · 56 minutes of footage

Every 10 seconds a new 20-second window closes. 16 frames go to the model; it answers with a class, a confidence and a sentence of reasoning.

75 / 100
marks had no training data
Train ships 5–30 s clips with one event. D2 and D3 are 4–10 minute videos with several. We synthesised 235, and deleted 125 more whose timestamps we could not verify.
5.5×
faster than real time
1.83 s to score a window that arrives every 10 s. Still 2.8× at the 95th percentile, so it holds on the tail.
4B
or 8B — both self-hosted
Indistinguishable on the practice set (3 videos of 24, p = 0.25). On the private eval pack the 8B produced our best submission. Nothing hosted is in the runtime path.
Demo: a dashboard runs this live — press a button, one window goes to the A100, the alert comes back in about nine seconds.
What happened

Short clips work. Long video does not —
and that is where the marks are.

First scored upload · zero-shot, no fine-tuning yet
23.8 / 100
24 videos, 20 positives → ±1 detection ≈ 5 pp of recall

Marks earned against marks available. One D3 video is worth ten D1 videos.

12 → 3
false alarms, for free
Keeping only the top answer per clip cut them 4× with no loss of recall, on CPU. The model's own confidence turned out to be useless as a filter.
7
silent failures, 0 crashes
Every one would have read as “the model isn't very good” — and the fix for the fifth caused the seventh. So we validate intermediate artifacts, not outputs.
0.556
AUC where chance is 0.500
The ego-motion gate every fixed-camera paper implies. We tested it against a permutation null: it does not work. A day of GPU saved.
Honest scope: no fine-tuned result existed at submission, so no fine-tuned number appears here. We retracted our own headline — “bigger is worse” was 3 videos out of 24, p = 0.25.
